9. SignalBridge — Deaf/Hard-of-Hearing Emergency Relay

Real-world problem: Deaf and hard-of-hearing individuals often can't use voice-based emergency services effectively, and text-to-911 support is inconsistent and slow in many regions.

Why now: Accessibility mandates are tightening globally, but the infrastructure gap for non-voice emergency communication remains largely unsolved at the coordination layer.

Why existing solutions are poor: Text-to-911 exists in patches but doesn't coordinate live interpretation support or reliably relay context-rich information (location, situation severity) the way voice calls do.

How it works:

Person in distress messages via a persistent Telegram/SMS channel.
Agent extracts key details (location, nature of emergency) and formats them for dispatch.
Relays formatted emergency info to a dispatcher-facing channel (email/Slack integration, depending on what the local service supports).
Simultaneously pulls in a volunteer sign-language-fluent interpreter via Discord for live relay support if video is involved.
Sends status updates back to the person via Telegram throughout.

Why multi-channel is essential: The person in crisis needs a channel they can actually use (text-based), dispatchers require structured formal reporting, and interpreters organize via community platforms — no single channel serves all three roles.

Why hard to think of: Most accessibility ideas stop at "chat interface for the deaf." The insight is coordinating a three-party real-time relay (person, interpreter, dispatcher) rather than a single conversation.

Innovation: 9
Technical difficulty: 8 (real emergency-service integration is genuinely hard and sensitive)
Winning potential: 7
15-day MVP: Build the relay logic with a mocked "dispatcher" endpoint (not real 911 integration, for obvious safety/legal reasons), demo the Telegram↔Discord interpreter routing convincingly.
Commercial potential: High social value; real deployment would require deep partnership with emergency services, so slower to commercialize but very strong as a mission-driven demo.
